# Handover is the transfer

Before, "full control at unlock" went to an account and a Bitcoin address the parent controls, so at 18 the parent still held keys that reached the money. Now the jar changes hands exactly once before adulthood, and every adult's access provably ends there.

- **Family account until handover.** The jar NFT is minted to a family account a parent manages. Before handover the rule runs (allowance, emergency payment) and nothing else, even after the unlock date: the unlock date is only the earliest handover.
- **The child sets up their own account.** About a week before unlock the child taps "Set up my jar" on their own phone. The phone makes a 12-word recovery phrase and derives from it both the NEAR key (the standard NEAR path) and a Bitcoin key (native segwit). The phrase is stored only on the phone, encrypted under Face ID or a PIN; no server keeps any copy or blob. The app creates a named sub-account whose only full-access key is that phone key, and funds it with a little NEAR so it never sees gas. A parent account on NEAR has no power over its sub-accounts, so neither the app nor any adult can use or recover it.
- **Guardians confirm, then a public delay.** The child's phone shows a short code derived from the account's key; each guardian compares it in person and confirms in their app. Once 2 guardians confirm, a public delay starts (3 days; minutes under demo timing). Any guardian can cancel during the delay, so one honest guardian stops an adult who swapped in their own account; a fresh claim can follow.
- **Anyone completes it.** After the delay and the unlock date, anyone may trigger handover. The contract builds the re-homing payment: every coin to a new jar address whose Bitcoin-only exit is the child's key, timelocked two years from then. Once that payment is proven confirmed, the NFT moves to the child's account. From the moment the delay ends the family account can request nothing, and after the move its next request fails with "not the holder".
- **The one recovery phrase.** ADR 0004 keeps seed phrases away from users; the child's account is the single exception, by product decision, because it is the only account nobody else may ever be able to restore. Adults' keys stay on their devices under Face ID or a PIN with no phrase shown; losing an adult's device never stops a handover, which needs only 2 guardians.
- **The handover window.** From the end of the delay no allowance or emergency payment runs and open emergency proposals lapse; completion waits for any pending payment. Re-homing may take several payments (at most 10 coins each), and the NFT moves once every coin the jar held when handover began has moved; a later stray is swept afterwards. Any guardian or the claimer can cancel an open claim until the coins start moving, and a claim nobody confirms lapses after 7 days.
- **Later transfers bind the receiver's own key.** The holder starts a transfer; the receiver's own account accepts it and supplies its Bitcoin key, so a seller can never choose the key that re-homed coins answer to. The holder can cancel a transfer until it is accepted.
- **No new parties.** No claim link an adult could use first, no login service that can restore the account. The README says plainly: after handover, nobody, us included, can recover the child's jar without their phone or their recovery phrase.
- **Later transfers** (a sale, a gift) use the same re-homing to the new holder's key, so one code path covers both.

## Considered Options

- Hand over the parent's login at 18: the parent keeps every key. Rejected; the old owner keeps access.
- Passkey sign-in with a synced passkey or server-stored blob: the passkey provider or our server becomes a party that can restore the account. Rejected for the child's account.
- Gas through relayed meta-transactions: no funded account needed, but more moving parts in the time left. Rejected; a small NEAR balance is simpler and gives no control.
