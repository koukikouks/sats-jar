What you are trusting

The rule keeps a jar's Bitcoin rate-limited until handover, and payable only to the beneficiary's address. Here is everyone that holds up.

NEAR's signing network
It signs the jar's Bitcoin payments. The contract only asks it to sign payments the rule allows.
NEAR's Bitcoin light client and its operators
The client checks that a top-up really happened on Bitcoin. Its operators submit the Bitcoin data and can pause or upgrade it.
The app's sponsor and keeper
They pay for accounts and fees and trigger the allowance each period. They cannot move coins outside the rule.
The app's code in your browser
The beneficiary's keys are made on their own device by this code, so "nobody else can use them" holds only if the code is honest. It is open source.
The guardians, at handover
If they collude, they could confirm a fake account as the beneficiary's.
A parent, while the beneficiary is a minor
The beneficiary's Bitcoin address comes from a wallet a parent controls until handover.
